Lentera, an iOS companion that reads, sees and walks with you. Lentera read · see · walk

A native iOS companion for blind and low-vision users. It reads text, recognises objects and detects obstacles through the camera, and speaks everything back in Indonesian.

Context

Every phone is full of screens, and screens assume sight. A blind user still reaches for a phone for its camera, its GPS and its voice assistant, but mainstream camera apps are built around a viewfinder the user cannot see.

Lentera turns the camera into a narrator. Point it at text, at an object or at the path ahead, and the phone describes what it finds in Indonesian, the language it was built around.

Lentera means lantern. The app works like one: it does not replace vision, it lights the way forward.

The problem

Reading a paragraph of scanned text requires the reading order to be right. Recognising an object requires a concise description, not a list of labels. Walking requires a sense of distance, not just names.

Three different jobs through one lens. The same camera frames have to feed all of them, instantly, without draining the battery or forcing the user to juggle separate apps.

The screen is optional when the camera can speak.

What I built

  1. 01

    Three assistive modes on one camera

    Text reading, object recognition and obstacle detection share one AVFoundation camera session. Reusing the session keeps resource overhead low and switching between modes nearly instant.

  2. 02

    A multimodal understanding pipeline

    Google Gemini reads camera frames, reconstructs a correct reading order for scanned text, and turns scenes into short spoken descriptions instead of raw labels.

  3. 03

    On-device OCR as an offline fallback

    Text is the most common need, so it also works without a network. The Vision framework reads the frame on device when there is no signal.

  4. 04

    A screen-reader-first interface

    Full VoiceOver support, Dynamic Type, and Indonesian output through AVSpeechSynthesizer. Accessibility is the interface, not a mode.

Decisions

Why describe instead of listing labels?
A screen reader user does not want to inspect five labels. One accurate spoken sentence is faster to understand and less tiring to hear.
Why keep an offline OCR path?
Cafes, streets and hospital corridors are not reliably online. Text reading is the most common need, so it is the one mode that must survive without a signal.
Why one camera session for three modes?
Starting and stopping capture between modes costs time and battery. One shared session makes switching feel immediate and keeps the phone cool.
Why Indonesian first?
The app was built for Indonesian users, so speech and labels live in Indonesian from the start rather than being bolted on later.

In one glance

  • 3 assistive modes on a single shared camera session
  • VO full VoiceOver and Dynamic Type support, screen-reader-first
  • ID Indonesian output through AVSpeechSynthesizer

Lentera is a personal project, so the real constraint was honesty: keep the scope tight enough to finish, and make accessibility the default rather than a setting.
